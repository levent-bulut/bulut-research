Narrative Gravity (Ng)

Anlatı Kütleçekimi

Narrative Gravity models the attractive force that certain narrative elements exert on plot, attention, and interpretation as a calculable property rather than an authorial trick. Developed by Levent Bulut within the Bulut Doctrine, it assigns mathematical mass to narrative elements in high-entropy systems and introduces the Vacuum Variable — proposing a deterministic break from Hitchcock's MacGuffin, in which the object of pursuit is arbitrary and interchangeable.

Core Constructs

  • Mathematical MassNarrative elements carry quantifiable mass proportional to the attention and causal weight they attract within the system.
  • Vacuum VariableThe structural void in a high-entropy system toward which narrative mass migrates — the calculable counterpart of unresolved tension.
  • Break from the MacGuffinWhere the MacGuffin is arbitrary and interchangeable, Ng makes the attractor determinate: its gravitational role follows from its mass, not from authorial fiat.
  • The Ng OperatorThe mathematical formalization and operational definition that turns Narrative Gravity from a metaphor into a computable quantity (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19275490).

From Determinism to Probabilistic Convergence

The doctrine's terminology was formally revised (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19164277): claims originally phrased as deterministic were restated as probabilistic convergence. Under the revised framework, Narrative Gravity does not dictate a single outcome; it defines the probability field toward which readings and plot trajectories converge. This revision applies across the doctrine and governs how Ng values are interpreted.

Interaction with Narrative Entropy

Narrative Gravity is defined for high-entropy systems: gravitational effects become measurable precisely where Narrative Entropy (Sn) is high and the system holds many open causal branches. As mass accumulates around an attractor, causal branching collapses and Sn falls — making Ng and Sn complementary quantities in the thermodynamics of narrative. The persistence of an attractor's influence over time is treated separately under Narrative Inertia.
