Narrative Inertia

Anlatı Ataleti

Narrative Inertia describes the persistence of established narrative trajectories: once a narrative system acquires momentum, its trajectory continues beyond the conditions that produced it. Developed by Levent Bulut within the Bulut Doctrine, it forms the temporal dimension of the framework together with Narrative Momentum (Nm), and extends from the individual reader's memory to the social propagation of narratives.

The Three Temporal Layers

Inertia operates across three connected layers of the doctrine, each documented in its own DOI-registered paper published in the same research cycle (April 2026):

  • Narrative Momentum (Nm)The temporal dimension of Objective Projection: the quantity a narrative trajectory carries forward from scene to scene (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19458013).
  • Narrative Memory Evolution (NME)How physical matrices persist, decay, and transform in reader memory after exposure — inertia at the individual level (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19462043).
  • Narrative Ecosystem Dynamics (NED)The social propagation layer: how narratives sustain trajectory as they move through populations and platforms (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19461954).

The Habituation Problem

Inertia has a limit condition. The Habituation Problem (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19648817) documents what happens under repeated exposure to a fixed physical matrix: the biophysical response attenuates, and the trajectory that inertia sustains loses its capacity to trigger the pre-cortical pathway. This defines the boundary between productive persistence and matrix exhaustion, and connects Narrative Inertia to the Scope Map of the doctrine (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19651674), which charts where Narrative Engineering works and where it does not.

Interaction with Gravity and Entropy

Within the doctrine's physical model, the three dynamic quantities are complementary: Narrative Gravity (Ng) defines where narrative mass attracts, Narrative Entropy (Sn) measures how disordered the system is, and Narrative Inertia determines how long an established trajectory resists change. A high-inertia narrative maintains its course even as entropy rises; a low-inertia narrative dissolves as soon as its gravitational anchor weakens.
